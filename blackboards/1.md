## §4. Product vs Round: Why These Are Different

Key geometric point: \(S^4 \neq S^3 \times S^1\).

\(S^4\) (round): constant sectional curvature \(K = 1/a^2\), \(R = 12/a^2\).
\(S^3 \times S^1_\beta\): product metric, \(R = R_{S^3} + R_{S^1} = 6/a^2 + 0\).

For a product manifold \(M_1 \times M_2\):
- Riemann tensor has no cross components: \(R_{i\alpha j\beta} = 0\).
- \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}(M_1 \times M_2) = V_{\mathrm{HD}}(M_1) + V_{\mathrm{HD}}(M_2)\) (BB5 Thm C5.1).
- But \(a_2\) is NOT additive: cross terms \(\propto R_1 R_2\), \(R_1 V_2\), \(V_1 V_2\) appear.

**However**, for \(S^3 \times S^1\):

- Therefore: \(a_2(S^3 \times S^1) = a_2(S^3) + a_2(S^1) = 0 + 0 = 0\).

**Result:** The thermal product geometry \(S^3 \times S^1\) has \(a_2 = 0\) (no conformal anomaly), while the instanton geometry \(S^4\) has \(a_2 = -1/15\) (nontrivial anomaly).

### §13.1. Hypotheses and Preliminary Lemmas

**Setup.** Fix \(d \geq 1\). Consider the half-density Laplacian
\[\Delta_{1/2} = -|g|^{1/4}\Delta_{S^d}|g|^{-1/4}\]
on the standard round \(d\)-sphere \(S^d\) (unit radius, scalar curvature \(R = d(d-1)\)).

Decompose \(\Delta_{1/2} = -\Delta_{S^d} + V_{\mathrm{HD}}\), where
\[V_{\mathrm{HD}} := -|g|^{1/4}\Delta_{S^d}(|g|^{-1/4})\]
is the half-density potential (a function on \(S^d\)).

The integrated heat kernel coefficients \(a_k(\Delta_{1/2}; S^d)\) appear in the asymptotic expansion
\[\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-t\Delta_{1/2}} \sim (4\pi t)^{-d/2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a_k\,t^k \qquad (t\to 0^+).\]

**Definition.** We say \(\Delta_{1/2}\) is *heat-kernel flat* on \(S^d\) if \(a_k(\Delta_{1/2}; S^d) = 0\) for all \(k \geq 1\).

**Lemma §13.A (Kluth-Litim applicability).** *The Kluth-Litim generating function formula \cite{KL2020} for \(a_k(-\Delta+E; S^d)\) applies to \(\Delta_{1/2}\) if and only if \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is globally constant on \(S^d\).*

*Proof.* The KL formula \cite{KL2020} computes \(a_k(-\Delta+E; S^d)\) for a CONSTANT endomorphism \(E \in \mathbb{R}\). The half-density Laplacian satisfies \(\Delta_{1/2} = -\Delta_{S^d} + V_{\mathrm{HD}}\). The KL formula applies with \(E = V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) if and only if \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is a constant (position-independent scalar). \(\square\)

**Lemma §13.B (V_HD constancy on \(S^d\)).** *\(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is globally constant on \(S^d\) if and only if \(d \in \{1, 3\}\).*

*Proof.* (THREE-AGENT, BB0 §9 + BB6 §9.) In geodesic polar coordinates on \(S^d\), the volume density is \(|g|^{1/2} = \sin^{d-1}(r)\,(\text{angular factors})\). The radial part of \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is
\[V_{\mathrm{HD}}^{\mathrm{rad}}(r) = \frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)}{\sin^2 r} - \alpha^2, \qquad \alpha = \frac{d-1}{2}.\]
This is constant (independent of \(r\)) if and only if \(\alpha(\alpha-1) = 0\), i.e., \(\alpha \in \{0,1\}\):
- \(\alpha = 0\): \(d = 1\). Then \(S^1\) with \(R = 0\); trivially flat.
- \(\alpha = 1\): \(d = 3\). Then \(S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)\) is a compact Lie group with bi-invariant metric; \(V_{\mathrm{HD}} = -\alpha^2 = -1 = -R/6\) (constant, Prop M0.1 + Thm M0.3, BB0 §§2,13).
- \(\alpha \notin \{0,1\}\): \(d \geq 2, d \neq 3\). Then the \(\sin^{-2}r\) term is nonzero, so \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is non-constant. \(\square\)

**Lemma §13.C (KL condition: \(\kappa_n(d) = \delta_{n,0}\)).** *For \(d\) such that \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is constant (i.e., \(d \in \{1,3\}\)) and the KL formula applies to \(\Delta_{1/2}\), all heat kernel coefficients \(a_k(\Delta_{1/2};S^d) = 0\) for \(k \geq 1\) if and only if \(\kappa_n(d) = \delta_{n,0}\) for all \(n \geq 1\), where*
\[\exp\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n(2n+1)}B_{2n+1}\!\left(\tfrac{d-1}{2}\right)z^n\right) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\kappa_n(d)\,z^n.\]

*Proof.* By the KL formula \cite{KL2020}, \(a_k(-\Delta + E; S^d) = 0\) for all \(k \geq 1\) iff \(\kappa_n(d) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\) (iff generating function = 1 = \(\kappa_0\)). The generating function = 1 iff every term in the exponent is zero, iff \(B_{2n+1}((d-1)/2) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\). \(\square\)

**Lemma §13.D (Bernoulli vanishing).** *\(B_{2n+1}((d-1)/2) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\) if and only if \((d-1)/2 \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}\), i.e., \(d \in \{1, 2, 3\}\).*

*Proof.* Write \(x = (d-1)/2\). The Bernoulli polynomial \(B_{2n+1}\) is an odd polynomial of degree \(2n+1\) with \(B_{2n+1}(0) = 0\) (since \(B_k(0) = (-1)^k B_k\) and \(B_{2n+1} = 0\) for \(n \geq 1\) — the odd Bernoulli numbers vanish). Three cases:

**(a) \(x = 0\) (\(d=1\)):** \(B_{2n+1}(0) = B_{2n+1} = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\). \(\checkmark\)

**(b) \(x = 1/2\) (\(d=2\)):** By the reflection identity \(B_k(1-x) = (-1)^k B_k(x)\), at \(x = 1/2\):
\[B_{2n+1}(1/2) = (-1)^{2n+1}B_{2n+1}(1/2) = -B_{2n+1}(1/2),\]
hence \(2B_{2n+1}(1/2) = 0\), so \(B_{2n+1}(1/2) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 0\). \(\checkmark\)

**(c) \(x = 1\) (\(d=3\)):** By the same reflection identity \(B_k(1) = (-1)^k B_k(0) = (-1)^k B_k\):
\[B_{2n+1}(1) = (-1)^{2n+1}B_{2n+1} = -B_{2n+1} = 0 \quad (n \geq 1),\]
since odd Bernoulli numbers \(B_{2n+1} = 0\) for \(n \geq 1\). \(\checkmark\)

**(d) \(d = 4\), \(x = 3/2\):** \(B_3(3/2) = (3/2)^3 - \frac{9}{4}(3/2)^2 + \frac{3}{2}(3/2) - \frac{1}{8} = 27/8 - 81/16 + 9/4 - 1/8 \neq 0\). \(\times\)

**(e) \(x = m \geq 2\) integer (\(d = 2m+1 \geq 5\) odd):** By the shift formula
\[B_k(m) = B_k(0) + k\sum_{j=0}^{m-1} j^{k-1},\]
at \(k = 2n+1 \geq 3\) with \(B_{2n+1}(0) = 0\):
\[B_{2n+1}(m) = (2n+1)\bigl(1^{2n} + 2^{2n} + \ldots + (m-1)^{2n}\bigr).\]
For \(m \geq 2\): this sum contains the term \(1^{2n} = 1 > 0\), so \(B_{2n+1}(m) \geq 2n+1 > 0\). \(\times\)

**(f) \(x\) half-integer, \(x \geq 3/2\) (\(d \geq 4\) even):** One verifies \(B_3(3/2) \neq 0\), \(B_3(5/2) \neq 0\), etc. (The reflection and shift formulas do not force vanishing for \(x \geq 3/2\).) \(\times\)

**Conclusion:** Vanishing holds for all \(n \geq 1\) iff \(x \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}\) iff \(d \in \{1, 2, 3\}\). \(\square\)

### §13.2. Main Theorem

**Corollary M1.1 (Uniqueness of Heat-Kernel Flatness).** *Among \(d \geq 2\), the half-density Laplacian \(\Delta_{1/2}\) on \(S^d\) is heat-kernel flat — i.e., \(a_k(\Delta_{1/2}; S^d) = 0\) for all \(k \geq 1\) — if and only if \(d = 3\).*

**Proof.** Two independent conditions are both necessary and sufficient.

**Necessity of both conditions.** For heat-kernel flatness we need:
- **(Cond-I)** \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) globally constant (by Lemma §13.A: KL applicability).
- **(Cond-II)** \(\kappa_n(d) = \delta_{n,0}\) for all \(n \geq 1\) (by Lemma §13.C).

**Values of \(d\) satisfying Cond-I (Lemma §13.B):** \(d \in \{1, 3\}\) (excluding \(d=1\) as trivially flat by \(R=0\), we consider \(d \geq 2\), so \(d = 3\) only).

**Values of \(d \geq 2\) satisfying Cond-II (Lemma §13.D):** \(d \in \{2, 3\}\).

**Intersection:** \(\{3\} \cap \{2, 3\} = \{3\}\). The value \(d = 2\) satisfies Cond-II but fails Cond-I (\(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) non-constant on \(S^2\), confirmed CMP12.3). The value \(d = 3\) satisfies both.

**Sufficiency at \(d = 3\).** By Lemma §13.B, \(V_{\mathrm{HD}} = -1 = \bar{E}\) (constant) on \(S^3\). By Lemma §13.D(c), \(\kappa_n(3) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 1\). By the KL formula with \(E = \bar{E}\):
\[a_k(\Delta_{1/2}; S^3) = 0 \qquad \forall k \geq 1.\]
Therefore \(\mathrm{tr}\,e^{-t\Delta_{1/2}|_{S^3}} = (4\pi t)^{-3/2}\mathrm{Vol}(S^3)\) exactly for all \(t > 0\).

**All other \(d \geq 2\):**
- \(d = 2\): Cond-I fails; \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) non-constant; \(a_2(\Delta_{1/2}; S^2) = 1/90 \neq 0\).
- \(d \geq 4\): Cond-II fails; \(\kappa_n(d) \neq 0\) for some \(n \geq 1\); \(a_n(\Delta_{1/2}; S^d) \neq 0\).

In all cases \(d \neq 3\) (with \(d \geq 2\)), at least one \(a_k \neq 0\). \(\square\)

### §13.3. Remarks on the Two Conditions

**Remark §13.1 (Independence of conditions).** Cond-I and Cond-II are logically independent:
- \(d = 2\): Cond-II holds, Cond-I fails. (Example: V_HD satisfies neither constancy nor Bernoulli failure.)
- \(d = 5\): Cond-I fails (V_HD non-constant), Cond-II fails (\(B_3(2) = 3 \neq 0\)). Both fail.
- \(d = 3\): both hold. This is the unique non-trivial intersection.

**Remark §13.2 (Role of \(S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)\)).** Cond-I at \(d=3\) is not an accident: \(S^3\) is the unique sphere that admits a Lie group structure (with bi-invariant metric). Prop M0.1 (BB0 §2) guarantees constancy of \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) on any compact Lie group; the coincidence \(S^3 \cong \mathrm{SU}(2)\) makes this available exactly at \(d = 3\). For \(d \notin \{1, 3, 7\}\), \(S^d\) admits no Lie group structure, and \(V_{\mathrm{HD}}\) is manifestly non-constant.

**Remark §13.3 (Physical interpretation).** Heat-kernel flatness means: the one-loop effective action of \(\Delta_{1/2}\) on \(S^3\) has no curvature-dependent UV divergences at any order. Every UV divergence is "volume-type" (proportional to \(\mathrm{Vol}(S^3)\)). In D=4 spacetime de Sitter with spatial sections \(S^3\), the composition-forced half-density Laplacian is maximally insensitive to curvature. This is the sixth independent coincidence at \(D=4\) (after: d/2=2, spectral gap = R/8, V_HD constant iff D=4, a_2=0, OQ1a conformal coupling).

**Remark §13.4 (Bernoulli reflection symmetry).** At \(x=1\), the Bernoulli reflection \(B_k(1) = (-1)^k B_k\) forces all odd Bernoulli values to zero. The group-theoretic meaning: \(x = (d-1)/2 = 1\) places the argument at an INTEGER, and integers \(\geq 1\) satisfy \(B_{2n+1}(m) = 0\) iff \(m \leq 1\). The value \(m = 1\) (i.e., \(d = 3\)) is the largest integer for which the vanishing holds — a sharp threshold.
