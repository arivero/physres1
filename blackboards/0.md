# Heat Kernel as Bi-Half-Density: Literature Scan

**Motivation:** motivations.md lists "heat kernel as bi-half-density" as the target worked
example for the half-density QFT paper. This note collects the literature foundations.

---

## S1. The Core Claim

The heat kernel K(t,x,y) on a Riemannian manifold (M,g) is not naturally a
function (bi-scalar). It is naturally a **bi-half-density**: a section of
|Omega|^{1/2}_x boxtimes |Omega|^{1/2}_y over M x M. The reason:

1. K(t,x,y) as written in coordinates transforms with factors of g(x)^{-1/4}
   and g(y)^{-1/4} under coordinate changes (it absorbs the sqrt-volume from
   both endpoints).
2. The semigroup law K(s+t,x,y) = int_M K(s,x,z) K(t,z,y) dz requires a
   measure at the intermediate point z. If K is a bi-half-density, the product
   K(s,x,z) K(t,z,y) in the z-slot pairs |dz|^{1/2} from each factor to produce
   a full density |dz|, enabling integration WITHOUT an external measure choice.
3. This is precisely the P4.2 composition mechanism in our framework.

## S2. Literature Sources

### S2.1. Melrose — Pseudodifferential operators on manifolds (MIT lecture notes)

**Source:** R. Melrose, "From Microlocal to Global Analysis" (2014 draft),
Chapter 6 (Pseudodifferential operators on manifolds).
URL: https://math.mit.edu/~rbm/18-157-S14/iml.pdf (PDF, not web-fetchable)

**Key content (from search summaries):**
- The Schwartz kernel of an operator A: C_c^inf(M) -> D'(M) is a distribution
  on M x M. When M has no canonical measure, the kernel is naturally a section
  of the density bundle Omega(M x M), not a function.
- If operators act on **half-densities** C^inf(M, |Omega|^{1/2}), then the
  kernel is a section of |Omega|^{1/2} boxtimes |Omega|^{1/2} over M x M.
- With this convention, "one need not specify a density with respect to which
  to integrate" — composition is canonical.
- The adjoint of a half-density operator has kernel obtained by swapping
  variables (no Jacobian factor), making self-adjointness transparent.

### S2.2. Bates-Weinstein — Lectures on the Geometry of Quantization (1997)

**Source:** S. Bates, A. Weinstein, "Lectures on the Geometry of Quantization,"
AMS Berkeley Math. Lecture Notes 8 (1997).
URL: https://math.berkeley.edu/~alanw/GofQ.pdf (PDF)

**Key content (from nLab summary):**
- Defines the half-density bundle |Omega|^{1/2} as the line bundle associated
  to GL(n) via the representation A -> |det A|^{-1/2}.
- The half-density bundle is the "square root" of the density bundle.
- For geometric quantization: rather than choosing a measure, one tensors the
  prequantum line bundle with |Omega|^{1/2}. Compactly supported sections can
  then be naturally integrated (inner product without measure choice).
- WKB states are half-densities on Lagrangian submanifolds (semiclassical
  states in the half-density formalism).

### S2.3. Hormander-Duistermaat — Fourier Integral Operators (1971-72)

**Source:** L. Hormander, Acta Math. 127 (1971); J.J. Duistermaat & L. Hormander,
Acta Math. 128 (1972).

**Key content:**
- FIO symbols are half-densities on canonical relations (Lagrangian submanifolds
  of T*(X) x T*(Y)).
- When FIO kernels are normalized as half-densities, stationary phase produces
  approximately unitary operators.
- Composition of FIOs corresponds to composition of canonical relations; the
  half-density normalization makes this composition natural.
- The Maslov bundle (metaplectic correction) is the obstruction to global
  definition of the half-density symbol.

### S2.4. Parker — Geodesics and Approximate Heat Kernels

**Source:** T.H. Parker, "Geodesics and Approximate Heat Kernels."
URL: https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/Geodesics&HK.pdf (PDF)

**Key content (from search):**
- Explicitly constructs the heat kernel acting on half-densities on M.
- The metric volume density sqrt(g) trivializes the half-density bundle (on a
  Riemannian manifold with a fixed metric, |Omega|^{1/2} is trivial via g^{1/4}).
- The pullback of the volume density via the exponential map gives the
  connection to the Van Vleck-Morette determinant.

### S2.5. Spectral geometry with fibred boundary metrics (Grieser et al.)

**Source:** Spectral geometry on manifolds with fibred boundary metrics II:
heat kernel asymptotics, Anal. Math. Phys. (2022).
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13324-022-00648-y

**Key content (from abstract):**
- Their Schwartz kernels are NOT multiplied with half-densities, which
  "simplifies the presentation but leads to some shifts in the asymptotics
  later on when studying compositions of Schwartz kernels."
- This is a negative-result confirmation: omitting half-densities creates
  artificial shifts in the heat kernel asymptotics. Including them removes
  the shifts — precisely because composition becomes canonical.

### S2.6. Visser — Van Vleck Determinants (hep-th/9303020)

**Source:** M. Visser, "van Vleck determinants: geodesic focusing and
defocusing in Lorentzian spacetimes," Phys. Rev. D 47 (1993) 2395.
arXiv: hep-th/9303020.

**Key content:**
- Defines the **scalarized** Van Vleck determinant:
  Delta(x,y) = (-1)^d det(nabla_mu^x nabla_nu^y sigma) / sqrt(g(x) g(y))
- The division by sqrt(g(x) g(y)) is precisely the passage from a bi-density
  to a bi-scalar. In reverse: the "raw" VVM determinant (without this division)
  is a bi-density of weight (1,1).
- Therefore sqrt(Delta_raw) is a bi-half-density of weight (1/2, 1/2).
- The heat kernel K(t,x,y) ~ (4 pi t)^{-d/2} Delta^{1/2}(x,y) sum a_k t^k
  is naturally a bi-half-density when Delta^{1/2} is taken in its raw
  (un-scalarized) form.

## S3. Synthesis: Why the Heat Kernel IS a Bi-Half-Density

**The argument in three steps:**

1. **Schwartz kernel theorem (half-density version):** Any continuous linear
   operator A: C^inf(M, |Omega|^{1/2}) -> D'(M, |Omega|^{1/2}) has a
   distributional kernel K_A in D'(M x M, |Omega|^{1/2} boxtimes |Omega|^{1/2}).
   (Melrose Ch.6, Hormander vol.1 Ch.5.)

2. **The heat operator e^{-t Delta_{1/2}} acts on half-densities.** On a
   Riemannian manifold, the Laplacian can be defined on half-densities via
   the conjugation Delta_{1/2} = g^{1/4} Delta g^{-1/4} (this is our O2).
   Its heat kernel is therefore a section of |Omega|^{1/2} boxtimes |Omega|^{1/2}.

3. **Composition = semigroup.** The semigroup law
   e^{-(s+t)Delta} = e^{-s Delta} e^{-t Delta}
   translates to kernel composition. In the half-density formulation, no
   external measure enters the composition integral — the pairing of
   half-densities at the intermediate point z produces the integration measure
   canonically. This is the heat-kernel version of P4.2.

**Connection to P4.2:** The worked example for the half-density QFT paper should
show explicitly that:
- Writing K(t,x,y) = (4pi t)^{-d/2} g(x)^{1/4} g(y)^{1/4} Delta_sc^{1/2}
  sum a_k t^k (where Delta_sc is the scalarized VVM det),
- The g^{1/4} factors ARE the half-density legs,
- Composition absorbs these factors: g(z)^{1/4} g(z)^{1/4} = g(z)^{1/2} = dvol(z),
- Yielding a measure-free composition law.

# Velhinho 2017 Path-Space Measure: Better Refs? + Recent Composable PI Measures

## V1. Current Reference: Velhinho 2017

[Velhinho2017InfDimMeasure] José Velhinho, "Topics of Measure Theory on Infinite
Dimensional Spaces," Mathematics 5(3) (2017), 44. DOI 10.3390/math5030044. OA: MDPI.
arXiv:2312.04365 (expanded 2023 version).

Used at H0.2a: "In infinite-dimensional spaces there is no nontrivial
translation-invariant sigma-finite Borel measure."

**Assessment:** Velhinho 2017 is a short MDPI review (a pedagogical survey, not
the original theorem). It correctly states the result but is not the primary source.

## V2. Better/More Authoritative References

The classical theorem has multiple independent proofs:

1. **Weil 1940**: A. Weil, "L'intégration dans les groupes topologiques et ses
   applications," Actualités Sci. Ind. 869, Hermann, Paris (1940). Proves: a
   locally compact group admits Haar measure iff it is locally compact. Converse:
   infinite-dimensional groups are NOT locally compact, hence no Haar measure.
   (Indirect argument.)

2. **Sudakov 1959**: V.N. Sudakov, "Linear sets with quasi-invariant measure,"
   Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR 127 (1959), 524-525. Proves: no nontrivial
   translation-invariant sigma-finite Borel measure on R^infinity or on any
   infinite-dimensional separable Banach space. (Direct proof, earliest explicit
   statement for linear spaces.)

3. **Oxtoby-Ulam**: Measure and category arguments for Polish groups.

4. **Glimm-Jaffe 1981/1987**: J. Glimm, A. Jaffe, "Quantum Physics: A Functional
   Integral Point of View," Springer (1987). Standard textbook treatment.

5. **Smolyanov-Montaldi 2017**: J. Montaldi, O.G. Smolyanov, "Feynman path
   integrals and Lebesgue-Feynman measures," Dokl. Math. 96 (2017), 368-372.
   arXiv:1612.06657. Defines "Lebesgue-Feynman measure" as a generalized
   (distributional) translation-invariant measure, formalizing what Dq DOES mean.

**Recommendation:** Replace or supplement Velhinho with:
- Sudakov 1959 (original theorem) or Glimm-Jaffe 1987 (standard physics textbook)
- Optionally add Smolyanov-Montaldi 2017 for the constructive side (what Dq IS)
- Velhinho can remain as a secondary/survey reference if desired

## V3. Recent Composable Path-Integral Measures (2024-2025)

### V3.1. Lackman (2024a): Groupoid Construction of Functional Integrals
arXiv:2402.05866. Formalizes Feynman's path integral via the pair groupoid.
Develops coordinate-free, partition-of-unity-free integration using the van Est
map + PL structure. Defines Wiener measure and stochastic integrals in this
framework. Applications: Chern-Simons (finite gauge group), Poisson sigma model.
**Composability:** Implicit in the groupoid structure (composition = groupoid
convolution). Already cited in our tangent-groupoid notebook.

### V3.2. Lackman (2024b): Mathematical Definition of Path Integrals on Symplectic Manifolds
arXiv:2406.14547. Rigorous path integral definition using pair groupoid on
symplectic manifolds. Composition law built in by construction.
Already cited in our tangent-groupoid notebook.

### V3.3. Lackman (2024c): Axiomatization of Path Integral Quantization
arXiv:2410.02739. Axiomatizes PI quantization, proves equivalence to Berezin
(coherent state) quantization. Composition axiom is central.

### V3.4. Bonanno-Falls-Ferrero (2025): Path Integral Measures and Diffeomorphism Invariance
arXiv:2503.02941. JHEP 2025. Shows diffeomorphism invariance constrains the PI
measure (redundancy between action and measure). Critiques non-invariant
Fradkin-Vilkovisky proposals. Concrete scalar-field examples with proper-time
cutoff. **Relevant to P4.2:** measure choice is not free but constrained by
symmetry — parallel to our claim that composition constrains the measure.

### V3.5. Obolenskiy (2025): Stochastic Approach to Path Integral Measure
arXiv:2511.15772 (Nov 2025, rev. Jan 2026). Proposes Lorentzian-time PI by
restricting to configuration manifold neighborhoods of classical trajectories,
fibrating to L^2-isometric flux spaces, and formulating as Gaussian-measure
expectation values. Connects to Euclidean PI via Feynman-Kac.

## V5. Bonanno-Falls-Ferrero 2025 Full Ingest (arXiv:2503.02941)

**Key result:** Diffeomorphism invariance constrains the PI measure. Fujikawa's
variable phi_F = g^{1/4} phi is explicitly identified as a **density of weight 1/2**.
The measure D(g^{1/4} phi) is invariant because phi_F transforms under orthogonal
operators (det = 1). Non-invariant measures (Fradkin-Vilkovisky with g^{00}) yield
coordinate-dependent divergences.

**Connection to P4.2:** Bonanno+ show symmetry (diffeomorphisms) forces the g^{1/4}
factor. We show composition forces it. These are INDEPENDENT arguments for the
same conclusion: the half-density measure is not a convention but is determined by
structural requirements. The action-measure redundancy (their §III.2) parallels
our observation that composition determines measure, not spectrum (BB1).

**Citable claim:** "Fujikawa's variable represents a density of weight one half"
(Bonanno-Falls-Ferrero 2025, JHEP). Direct confirmation of our g^{1/4} = half-density.
